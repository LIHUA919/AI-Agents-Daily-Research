### [1] [Ovis-Embedding: Pushing the Frontiers of Universal Omni-Modal Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25165)
*Ovis-Embedding Team*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: Ovis-Embedding is a state-of-the-art omni-modal embedding family that uses a shared multimodal backbone to encode text, image, video, and audio in a common representation space, achieving SOTA performance across multiple multimodal evaluation benchmarks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To overcome modality fragmentation and create universal embedding models for any-to-any retrieval by developing a unified approach that handles multiple modalities without separate modality-specific towers.

Method: Three key advances: (1) native omni-modal initialization using pretrained Qwen-omni model with contrastive training and low-rank initialization; (2) data-centric training with broad multimodal corpus and homogeneous-source sampling for better data efficiency; (3) embedding-specific optimization with focal loss for hard examples and similarity-based Embedding Distillation.

Result: Achieves state-of-the-art performance on MMEB-v3, MMEB-v2, MVEB, MAEB, and RTEB benchmarks across text, image, video, and audio modalities.

Conclusion: Demonstrates the effectiveness of unified omni-modal training and highlights the potential for overcoming modality fragmentation to advance universal embedding models for any-to-any retrieval tasks.

Abstract: In this report, we introduce \textbf{Ovis-Embedding}, a state-of-the-art omni-modal embedding family built on native integration of text, image, video, and audio. Instead of assembling separate modality towers, Ovis-Embedding uses a shared multimodal backbone to encode different modalities in a common representation space. Specifically, we make \textbf{three key advances}: (1) \textbf{native omni-modal initialization}: we adopt a pretrained Qwen-omni model as the embedding backbone and adapt it through contrastive training with low-rank initialization; (2) \textbf{data-centric omni-modal training}: we construct a broad, high-quality corpus spanning text, images, video, audio, and interleaved multimodal data. To improve data efficiency, we introduce homogeneous-source sampling to form task-consistent batches with informative in-batch negatives; and (3) \textbf{embedding-specific training and inference optimization}: we use focal loss to emphasize hard examples and similarity-based Embedding Distillation to transfer fine-grained similarity structure from complementary experts. At inference time, low-rank feature decomposition enables compact embeddings with flexible dimensionality and minimal performance loss. Empirical evaluations show that the \textbf{Ovis-Embedding} family achieves state-of-the-art performance on \textbf{MMEB-v3}, \textbf{MMEB-v2}, \textbf{MVEB}, \textbf{MAEB}, and \textbf{RTEB}, demonstrating its effectiveness across text, image, video, and audio modalities. These results highlight the potential of unified omni-modal training to overcome modality fragmentation and advance universal embedding models for any-to-any retrieval.

### [4] [The Tasteful Agent: Measuring and Improving Taste in Long-Horizon Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25804)
*Wenbo Pan,Zhichao Liu,Shujie Liu,Jingying Zeng,Chin-Yew Lin,Xianfeng Tang,Yan Lu,Qi He,Xiaohua Jia*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: Taste-Bench is a new benchmark that evaluates LLM agents' "taste" - their ability to make good long-horizon decisions - by testing them on decision forks mined automatically from agent trajectories.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing benchmarks only measure end-to-end success of LLM agents on long-horizon tasks, but none specifically evaluates their ability to make good intermediate decisions (their "taste"), which is crucial for engineering and research agents.

Method: Built Taste-Bench automatically from agent trajectories by mining decision forks from parallel attempts at tasks and detours within trajectories. Each question presents a decision point where multiple directions are available, and models must choose the best path without seeing what happens after the fork.

Result: Best model achieved only 59.7% accuracy, forks with later-appearing evidence were harder for all models, reasoning budget didn't improve accuracy, but taste can be trained via distillation from a teacher model that has seen outcomes.

Conclusion: Taste is a measurable and trainable capability for LLM agents; teaching models to make better long-horizon decisions improves their performance on unseen tasks and end-to-end success rates.

Abstract: LLM agents increasingly work on long-horizon tasks, and the decisions they make along the way, such as which hypothesis to test or which implementation to build on, determine the outcome of the whole run. Making these decisions well is becoming a key capability for both engineering and research agents. We refer to the ability to make good long-horizon decisions as the taste of an agent. While existing benchmarks measure the end-to-end success of agents on long-horizon tasks, none of them measures the taste of an agent. To address this problem, we build Taste-Bench, a benchmark of taste questions constructed automatically from trajectories that agents produced in engineering and research tasks. Each question presents a decision fork, a point in a trajectory where multiple directions are available and one of them leads to a better outcome, and the evaluated model chooses among these directions without seeing what happens after the fork. We mine these forks automatically from parallel attempts at the same task and from detours inside a single trajectory, without needing human annotation. We evaluate frontier models on Taste-Bench and find that the best model answers only 59.7% of the questions correctly. We further find that forks whose deciding evidence appears later in the trajectory are much harder for every model, and that a larger reasoning budget does not improve the accuracy. Finally, we show that taste can be trained. We distill the judgment of a teacher that has seen the outcome into a student model, and the student makes better decisions on unseen tasks and improves end-to-end success on held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks.

### [5] [Targeted Review for AI-Assisted Biodiversity Surveys: Active Continuous-Score Occupancy Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25657)
*Timm Haucke,Lauren Harrell,Justin Kay,Mary Clapp,Sara Beery*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: ACORN is a method that strategically selects ML-generated labels for expert review to maximize ecological inference accuracy while minimizing review effort, outperforming non-targeted review policies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Scientists increasingly use ML to label scientific datasets, but ML models are imperfect and errors can propagate into scientific understanding with systematic bias. While scientists review ML-generated labels to ensure findings aren't biased, current review approaches are inefficient and don't optimally allocate reviewing effort relative to scientific goals.

Method: ACORN (Active Continuous-Score Occupancy Modeling) incorporates ML predictions into occupancy models and strategically selects samples for expert review that are maximally informative for downstream ecological analysis, rather than optimizing for classifier accuracy alone.

Result: Across camera-trap and bioacoustic datasets, ACORN recovers ecological conclusions close to those obtained from fully human-labeled data while requiring substantially fewer expert reviews than non-targeted review policies.

Conclusion: ML-assisted scientific workflows should optimize expert effort for downstream inference rather than classifier accuracy alone, especially when human review budgets are limited. Active learning approaches like ACORN can significantly improve efficiency in scientific labeling tasks.

Abstract: We increasingly use machine learning to label scientific datasets. The models we develop and deploy are improving all the time, but they are not and will likely never be perfect. Mistakes matter, as errors can propagate into our scientific understanding, particularly when systematically biased. Very reasonably, scientists thus review substantial proportions of ML-generated labels to verify or correct mistakes in pursuit of ensuring their scientific findings are not biased by ML. In this work, we focus on helping scientists optimally allocate this reviewing effort relative to their scientific goals. We focus on a specific class of scientists (ecologists) and a specific, widespread, and impactful modeling target (occupancy modeling, which estimates where species are likely to occur, conditioned on environmental factors). We introduce Active Continuous-Score Occupancy Modeling (ACORN), a method that incorporates ML predictions into occupancy models and strategically selects samples for expert review that are maximally informative for downstream ecological analysis. Across camera-trap and bioacoustic datasets, our method recovers ecological conclusions close to those obtained from fully human-labeled data, while requiring substantially fewer expert reviews than non-targeted review policies. Our results suggest that ML-assisted scientific workflows should optimize expert effort for downstream inference, rather than for classifier accuracy alone, especially when human review budget is limited. ### [6] [Latest Exact Match Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25802)
*Moritz Brösamle*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: LEMA is a new attention variant that binarizes queries/keys and only attends to latest exact matches, enabling efficient transformers with growing state that can theoretically simulate word-RAMs and vice versa.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The paper aims to develop a computationally efficient transformer attention mechanism that maintains theoretical expressiveness while enabling practical benefits like constant-time inference and efficient memory use. It addresses limitations of both standard softmax attention (quadratic cost) and other hard attention variants (computational complexity).

Method: Proposes LEMA (Latest Exact Match Attention) where queries and keys are binarized, and each query only attends to the most recent exactly matching key. Uses a training method with straight-through estimator for binarization and soft attention surrogate annealed toward LEMA. Also implements dictionary-based inference for constant generation speed.

Result: Proves LEMA transformers with chain-of-thought can simulate word-RAMs, and word-RAMs can simulate LEMA transformers with cost per token independent of context length. On synthetic associative recall, LEMA outperforms gated DeltaNet. In language modeling, LEMA matches half-sized softmax transformers in loss but lags on rare phrase tasks while recalling longer distances than comparable GDN models. Achieves constant generation speed with dictionaries in main memory.

Conclusion: LEMA establishes close theoretical correspondence between transformers and word-RAMs while offering practical advantages: efficient computation, constant-time inference despite growing state, and competitive performance on certain tasks. It represents a promising direction for scalable, theoretically-grounded transformer architectures.

Abstract: We introduce latest exact match attention (LEMA), an attention variant for transformers where queries and keys are binarized and each query attends only to the latest exactly matching key. We prove that LEMA transformers with chain of thought can simulate word-RAMs, as was recently shown for the less restrictive rightmost hard attention. In contrast to prior hard attention variants, the restriction to exact matches enables an efficient converse direction: word-RAMs can simulate LEMA transformers at a cost per token independent of the context length. Together, these results yield a close correspondence between the two computational models in terms of both compute and memory. Beyond the theory, we propose a training method for LEMA transformers that handles their non-differentiable operations with a straight-through estimator for the binarization and a soft attention surrogate annealed towards LEMA. On a synthetic associative recall task, LEMA models trained this way use their growing state to store and recall a large number of associations, outperforming gated DeltaNet (GDN) with its fixed state size. As a first scaling test, we train LEMA language models with up to 834 million parameters. They match softmax transformers of around half their size in loss and, on repeated rare phrases and a needle-retrieval task, remain behind softmax transformers but recall across longer distances than GDN models of comparable size. Finally, we implement dictionary-based inference for LEMA transformers and show constant generation speed comparable to GDN despite their growing state, with the dictionaries residing in main memory rather than VRAM. Code is available at https://github.com/moritzbroe/latest_exact_match_attention.

### [7] [DeepFEAv2: Deep Learning for Transient Finite Element Analysis Beyond Structured Meshes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26426)
*Georgios Triantafyllou,Panagiotis G. Kalozoumis,Dimitris K. Iakovidis*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: DeepFEAv2 is an improved deep learning surrogate framework that predicts transient finite element analysis simulations across different mesh topologies and element types, overcoming limitations of previous structured-mesh-only approaches.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Traditional FEA has high computational costs limiting real-time and high-resolution applications. Existing deep learning surrogates are often restricted to steady-state prediction or structured meshes, and cannot jointly predict node- and element-based outputs over time.

Method: DeepFEAv2 includes: (a) a module using FE connectivity matrix to organize input features by element into input sequences guided by mesh topology; (b) novel neural network architecture to process these sequences and jointly predict NEO over time; (c) FEA-informed optimization strategy for regularizing NEO predictions.

Result: Achieved R² values up to 0.99 and normalized errors as low as 0.38% on structured/unstructured 3D linear elastic datasets and pressure-driven aortic valve dataset. Outperformed DeepFEA with up to 38.0% relative increase in R² and 87.1% reduction in error. Inference was up to 1000× faster than traditional FEA.

Conclusion: DeepFEAv2 efficiently models transient FEA simulations across complex FE settings, providing a scalable surrogate framework for transient FEA that works with various mesh topologies and element types.

Abstract: Finite Element Analysis (FEA) is widely used for transient mechanical simulations, but its high computational cost limits real-time and high-resolution applications. Deep learning surrogate models can reduce this cost; however, many existing approaches are restricted to steady-state prediction or cannot jointly predict Node- and Element-based Outputs (NEO) over time. The state-of-the-art DeepFEA framework has addressed these issues but remains limited to structured finite element (FE) meshes. To overcome this limitation, this study proposes DeepFEAv2, a deep learning surrogate framework that enables prediction of transient FEA simulations across different FE mesh topologies and element types. The main contributions of DeepFEAv2 are: (a) a module that uses the FE connectivity matrix to organize input features by element and arrange them into an input sequence guided by the mesh topology; (b) a novel neural network architecture designed to process the input sequence and jointly predict NEO over time; and (c) a FEA-informed optimization strategy for regularizing these NEO predictions. DeepFEAv2 was evaluated on structured and unstructured 3D linear elastic datasets, as well as on a pressure-driven aortic valve dataset. DeepFEAv2 achieved R^2 values up to 0.99 and normalized errors as low as 0.38%. Compared with DeepFEA, it achieved up to 38.0% relative increase in R^2 and up to 87.1% reduction in normalized error. DeepFEAv2 also performed inference up to three orders of magnitude faster than traditional FEA. These results demonstrate that DeepFEAv2 can efficiently model transient FEA simulations across increasingly complex FE settings, providing a scalable surrogate framework for transient FEA.

</details>
